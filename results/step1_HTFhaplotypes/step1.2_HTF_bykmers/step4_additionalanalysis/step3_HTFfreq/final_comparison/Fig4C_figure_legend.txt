Fig. 4C.  OBC carriage across HTF haplotypes and geographic strata
in historical and modern P. viridiflava.

========================================================================
VERSION 1 — LONG (detailed; for supplementary or methods)
========================================================================

(Upper panel)  Frequency of each of the four major HTF haplotypes — 1803
and 1245 (OBC-positive; OBC+) and 1383 and 1830 (OBC-negative; OBC−) —
among Historical (pre-antibiotic era; n = 38) and Modern (n = 1312)
P. viridiflava isolates. Within the Historical era, OBC+
haplotypes (1803 + 1245) accounted for 21 of 38 isolates (55.26%).
The distribution of the four haplotypes was compared against a uniform
25% expectation within each era (chi-square goodness-of-fit: Historical chi-square goodness-of-fit chi2 = 0.95, df = 3, p = 0.814 [ns];
Modern chi2 = 607.1, df = 3, p = 3.0 × 10⁻¹³¹ [***]).

(Middle panel)  OBC carriage frequency (OBC+ and OBC−) across all isolates
in each era (Historical n = 38, Modern n = 1312). OBC+ isolates comprised
55.26% (21/38) of Historical and 83.69% (1098/1312) of Modern isolates.
OBC+ carriage differed from equal split (50%) in both eras
(Historical: exact binomial p = 0.627 [ns]; Modern: exact binomial p = 2.3 × 10⁻¹⁴³ [***]).

(Supplementary Fig.)  Downsampling OBC frequency analysis.

(A)  OBC frequency in Germany Historical isolates (n = 8;
2/8 = 25% OBC+; Wilson 95% confidence intervals).
Exact binomial test (H₀: OBC+ = 50%) yielded p = 0.289 [ns],
indicating no significant deviation from equal OBC+/− split in this dataset.

(B)  To assess OBC frequency differences between Historical and Modern
datasets without bias from geographic origin and sample size, the Modern pool
(n = 1312) was downsampled to match the Germany Historical sample size
(n = 8). 1000 independent runs were performed, each drawing n = 8
isolates without replacement. OBC+ and OBC− proportions per run are shown
as a boxplot (IQR with median line, whiskers 1.5×IQR) with individual
run proportions overlaid as jittered points.

(C)  Distribution of two-sided exact binomial p-values across 1000
downsampling runs. Within each run, OBC frequency was assessed by exact
binomial test (H₀: OBC+ = 50%%). Red dashed line: α = 0.05.
Only 21.9% of runs yielded binomial p < 0.05 (median p = 0.070),
indicating that n = 8 samples from the Modern dataset do not reliably
represent the true Modern OBC frequency. Accordingly, there is insufficient
power to perform a cross-dataset OBC frequency comparison, and no such
comparison is reported.
