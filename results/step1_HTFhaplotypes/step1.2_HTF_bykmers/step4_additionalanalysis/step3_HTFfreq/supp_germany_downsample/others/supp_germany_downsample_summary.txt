STATISTICAL APPROACH
--------------------
OBC carriage was assessed within each era using two complementary tests:

  (1) Four-group HTF haplotype composition (upper panel):
      Chi-square goodness-of-fit test — are the four HTF haplotypes
      (1803, 1245, 1383, 1830) equally distributed within each era?
      H₀: each group = 25%.

  (2) Binary OBC+/− carriage (middle panel):
      Two-sided exact binomial test within each era —
      does OBC+ frequency differ from 50%%? H₀: OBC+ = 50%%.

WHY NO CROSS-ERA COMPARISON WAS PERFORMED
------------------------------------------
Comparing Germany Historical (n = 8) against Modern isolates
requires downsampling the Modern pool (n = 1312) to n = 8 to achieve
sample-size parity. At such small n, the resulting proportion carries
substantial sampling variance and cannot serve as a reliable reference.

To quantify this variance, we performed 1000 independent downsample runs:
in each run, n = 8 isolates were drawn without replacement from the
Modern pool, and a two-sided exact binomial test was applied to the
sampled observations (H₀: OBC+ = 50%).
Across 1000 runs, only 21.9% yielded p < 0.05 (median p = 0.070).
In the vast majority of runs, a random sample of n = 8 from the
Modern pool could not be distinguished from a 50/50 split, indicating
insufficient information to yield a reliable Modern reference proportion.
Accordingly, no cross-era comparison is reported.

METHODS
-------
Confidence intervals (Panel A): Wilson score 95%% CI,
  binom::binom.confint(k, n, method='wilson'). Preferred over
  Clopper-Pearson for small n: better coverage, less conservative.

Downsampling (Panels B, C): set.seed(42);
  1000 independent downsample runs of n=8 isolates drawn
  without replacement from Modern pool (n=1312). Per run:
  two-sided exact binomial test (H₀: OBC+ = 50%).
  No cross-era comparison is reported (see rationale above).

Significance thresholds: *** p<0.001 | ** p<0.01 | * p<0.05 | ns p≥0.05
